# KRS College Connect — Brand & UX Rules

## Guiding philosophy

**Fisher Price simple.** Press a button, something happens. No dropdowns inside dropdowns. No jargon. A 15-year-old football player and their 45-year-old parent both need to get value in under a minute without reading a manual.

## Audience

Primary users are high school football athletes (ages 14–18) and their parents. Secondary users are club administrators at high school football programs who licensed the platform. Tertiary users are college coaches who may receive athlete outreach.

Athletes are visual, mobile-first, and will bounce if anything feels corporate or tired. Parents want to feel their money is well-spent. Admins want confidence they can run their club's recruiting program without a support ticket.

## Brand voice

- Confident but not cocky. This platform helps athletes tell their own story to coaches — it doesn't promise recruitment outcomes.
- Plain language. No "empower your journey" / "unlock your potential" garbage.
- Respect the athlete's intelligence. They know football. They don't know the college process. Explain that, not the sport.
- No hype emojis. Emojis are fine where they aid scannability (⚽ next to a schedule item), not as decoration.

## Visual identity

- Pilot club is Mercer Island High School Football. Their colors — deep navy and crimson — are the baseline for the Eastside tenant.
- Every club has configurable primary color, secondary color, logo URL. Athletes see THEIR club's branding, not generic KRS branding.
- Typography: a crisp geometric sans for headlines, a humanist sans for body. Avoid overdone display fonts. 
- Generous whitespace. High contrast. No gradient-on-gradient "AI dashboard" aesthetic.

## Anti-patterns — things that read as AI slop

- Vercel branding visible anywhere in the product
- Lorem ipsum or "John Doe" placeholder content left in
- Generic Shadcn defaults with no customization
- Over-use of glassmorphism, neon gradients, or floating 3D shapes
- Stock photos of smiling teens
- Any copy that sounds like it was generated by asking "write marketing copy for a recruiting app"

## Interaction rules

- Primary actions are large and obvious (Save to Pipeline, Add Game, Upload Highlight).
- Destructive actions require confirmation (Delete School, Remove Coach).
- Errors speak in plain language: "Couldn't save this school. Try again?" — never "Error 42703: column does not exist".
- Loading states always shown for any operation over ~300ms.
- Forms save progress. An athlete who's halfway through the School Fit Quiz and navigates away should be able to pick up where they left off.

## Copy patterns

- "Add to Pipeline" not "Save this school for future reference"
- "Sign Out" not "Log out of your session"
- "My Schools" not "Your Recruiting Pipeline"
- "Highlights" not "Multimedia Content"

## Mobile-first

Most athlete usage is on phone. Every page designed for 375px width first, then scaled up. Tap targets are 44px minimum. No hover-only interactions.

## Accessibility baseline

- Semantic HTML
- Color contrast ratio ≥ 4.5:1 for body text
- All form fields have labels, not just placeholders
- Keyboard navigation works everywhere
- Focus states are visible

## Formatting rules Claude must follow when writing copy or content for KRS

- No bullet lists in product UI copy. Write in short sentences.
- No "Let's dive in!" or conversational AI tropes.
- No emojis in product UI unless they serve an informational purpose.
- Never use phrases like "seamlessly" or "effortlessly" in product copy. If it's true, the user will feel it without being told.

## Club-specific branding

Each tenant has:
- `name` — full club name (Mercer Island High School Football)
- `short_name` — header-friendly (Mercer Island Football)
- `logo_url` — square crest
- `primary_color` — hex
- `secondary_color` — hex
- `locale` — city/state (Seattle, WA)

The Club Admin dashboard header displays club name + crest. Athlete dashboard shows "YOUR_CLUB_NAME Recruiting Platform" in the left sidebar, not "KRS College Connect."

The "KRS College Connect" branding shows only in: the landing page, the marketing site, billing emails, admin-level terms of service. Athletes should feel they're using THEIR club's app.
